\section{Geometric Integration}
Traditional numerical integration methods such as the Runge-Kutta or Verlet schemes 
are limited in their accuracy, as they do not inherently include the conservation principles. 
Physical systems, under certain circumstances have quantities that are conserved, such as 
angular/linear momentum, symplectic form, and other geometric structures that exist in the configuration space. 
Geometric integrators are developed to preserve these structures, allowing for long time accuracy in its qualitative behaviour. 

\subsection{Lie Group Variational Integrators}
The attitude of a rigid body can be described by a rotation matrix $R \in SO(3) = \{ R \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3} \;\mid\; R^\top R = I,\; \det(R) = 1 \}$.
The set of rotation matrices $SO(3)$, is an example of a Lie Group. The attitude and position of a rigid body can be described by the Lie Group $SE(3)$.
The discrete equations of motion used for the numerical integration schemes are derived
by discretising the Lagrangian, and then taking a discrete variational approach to Hamilton's Principle 
to find the equations of motion. 

\subsection{Continuous Time Equations of Motion}
For our spacecraft, we can model the system by the following continuous time equations of motion. 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x} &= v \\
m \dot{v} &= - \nabla U(x) + F_T + F_D \\
\dot{R} &= R S(\Omega) \\
\dot{\Pi} + \Omega \times \Pi &= M + \tau_T + \tau_D
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

$U(x)$ is the potential energy function around Mars, $F_T$ and $F_D$ are forces 
from thrust and drag, and $\tau_T$ and $\tau_D$ are corresponding torque quantites. 
$M$ represents the gravity gradient torque. 
$R$ is the rotation matrix from body-fixed frame to the inertial frame of reference, which has its origin at the center of Mars.
$S(.)$ mapping produces the skew symmetric matrix upon acting on a vector, as the following: 
\begin{equation}
S(x) = 
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & -x_3 & x_2 \\
x_3 & 0 & -x_1 \\
-x_2 & x_1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
$\Pi$ represents the angular momentum, and $\Omega$ represents the angular velocity, both in the body fixed frame.  
The force produced from the fuel ejecting and mass decreasing is not modelled. 

\subsection{Discrete Equations of Motion}
The discrete equations of motion in the Hamiltonian form: 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
x_{k+1} &= x_k + \frac{h}{m} \gamma_k + \frac{h^2}{2m} \left( -\nabla U(x_k) + f(x_k, p_k) \right)\\
p_{k+1} &= p_k + \frac{h}{2}  \left( -\nabla U(x_k) + f(x_k, p_k) \right) \\
&+ \frac{h}{2}  \left( -\nabla U(x_{k+1}) + f(x_{k+1}, p_{k+1}) \right) \\
h S(\Pi_k + \frac{h}{2}(M_k + \tau_k)) &= F_k J_d + J_d F_k ^T \\
\Pi_{k+1} &= F_k^T \Pi_k + \frac{h}{2} F_k^T (M_k + \tau_k) + \frac{h}{2} (M_{k+1} + \tau_{k+1}) \\
R_{k+1} &= R_k F_k
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Equation (3a) can be solved by considering the Lie Algebra of $F_k$, and using a newton iteration solver. 
In the various papers by M Leok, it is explained why $F_k$ always remains on $SO(3)$. 

\section{Geometric Attitude Control}
The control inputs, are the 4 throttle inputs for each of the engines. The torque produced by the thrusters 
can be found as shown in the extension pack. Thus, we consider the desired moment that needs to be produced by the engines, 
as we can simply invert the matrix that relates the torque to the throttle values. 
As shown in the paper by T.Lee et al (Geometric tracking control of a quadrotor UAV on SE(3)), 
we can define the error in attitude not by naviely subtracting the euler angles, but rather intrinsically on $SO(3)$.

The error in attitude becomes:
\begin{equation}
    e_R = \frac{1}{2} \left(R_d ^T R -  R^T R_d \right)^{\vee}
\end{equation}

The $(.)^{\vee}$ map is the inverse of the $S(.)$ map. 
The error in angular velocity then is defined to be: 
\begin{equation}
    e_{\Omega} = \Omega - R^T R_d \Omega_d
\end{equation}

Then the control input, or desired torque is: 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_u = &-k_R e_R - k_{\Omega} e_{\Omega} + \Omega \times J \Omega \\
       &+ - J \big( S(\Omega) R^{T} R_{d} \Omega_{d} - R^{T} R_{d} \dot{\Omega}_{d} \big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
